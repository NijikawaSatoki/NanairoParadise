Vowel length

There are two vowel length marks; however, only one of them is considered to be “correct”, while the other is historical. So, the long vowels are represented with a dot above (dot below in the case of i). For example:
ȧ /ɑː/, æ̇ /ɛː/, ė /eː/, ị /iː/, ŀ̇ /l̩ː/, ȯ /ɔː/, ø̇ /øː/, ơ̇ /ɤː/, ƣ̇ /oːi̯/, ɍ̇ /r̩ː/, u̇ /uː/, and ư̇ /yː/.

The vowel ŭ is always short.

Historically, long vowels were represented with a double acute accent (except for with certain tones, more on that later). Once again, as an example:
a̋ /ɑː/, æ̋ /ɛː/, e̋ /eː/, i̋ /iː/, ŀ̋ /l̩ː/, ő /ɔː/, ø̋ /øː/, ơ̋ /ɤː/, ƣ̋ /oːi̯/, ɍ̋ /r̩ː/, ű /uː/, and ư̋ /yː/.

The reason for switching the length mark to the dot above (or below) was to improve readability and maintain orthographic consistency, especially when the tone marks come into play.

As a note, when placing the length and tone marks together, the length mark is placed above the tone mark (if the tone is any of them but low tone), or below for low tone i.

About ŀ and ɍ

The letters ŀ and ɍ are not vowels, despite being counted amongst the vowels, but rather are syllabic consonants. These types of consonants are simply a consonant (usually the sonorants /l/ and /r/) that takes the place of the vowel.

Tone marks

Neph has 8 tones that a syllable can have. They are high, mid, low, rising, falling, peaking, dipping, and transition.

High tone, known in Neph as tayŋdyƣ̃ƿƿi̱̣j (pronounced /tɑjŋ˧djoi̯w˦˨.wʲiːd͜ʒ˩/), is written as a macron above the vowel. For example: ā, ǣ, ē, ī, ŀ̄, ō, ø̄, ơ̄, ƣ̄, ɍ̄, ū, ư̄, and ŭ̄.

Mid tone, known in Neph as tayŋdyƣ̃ƿƿùƶ (pronounced /tɑjŋ˧djoi̯w˦˨.wud͜z˥˩/), is not written at all. It is considered to be the default tone of a syllable. For example: a, æ, e, i, ŀ, o, ø, ơ, ƣ, ɍ, u, ư, and ŭ.

Low tone, known in Neph as tayŋdyƣ̃ƿƿø̂̇ð (pronounced /tɑjŋ˧djoi̯w˦˨.wøːð˧˦˧/), is written as a macron below the vowel. For example: a̱, æ̱, e̱, i̱, ŀ̱, o̱, ø̱, ơ̱, ƣ̱, ɍ̱, u̱, ư̱, and ŭ̱.

Rising tone, known in Neph as tayŋdyƣ̃ƿƿɍ́f (pronounced /tɑjŋ˧djoi̯w˦˨.wr̩f˩˥/), is written as an acute accent. For example: á, ǽ, é, í, ŀ́, ó, ǿ, ớ, ƣ́, ɍ́, ú, ứ, and ŭ́. This tone used to be written differently for long vowels. It was written as an acute accent (to indicate the rising tone), and the length marker was written as a hook above. Those former spellings are as follow: á̉, ǽ̉, é̉, í̉, ŀ́̉, ó̉, ǿ̉, ớ̉, ƣ́̉, ɍ́̉, ú̉, and ứ̉.

Falling tone, known in Neph as tayŋdyƣ̃ƿƿŀm (pronounced /tɑjŋ˧djoi̯w˦˨.wl̩m˧/), is written as a grave accent. For example: à, æ̀, è, ì, ŀ̀, ò, ø̀, ờ, ƣ̀, ɍ̀, ù, ừ, and ŭ̀. This tone used to be written differently for long vowels. It was written as a double grave accent, to represent both the falling tone and the long vowel. Those former spellings are as follow: ȁ, æ̏, ȅ, ȉ, ŀ̏, ȍ, ø̏, ơ̏, ƣ̏, ɍ̏, ȕ, and ư̏.

Peaking tone, known in Neph as tayŋdyƣ̃ƿƿǣ̇p (pronounced /tɑjŋ˧djoi̯w˦˨.wɛːp˥/), is written as a circumflex. For example: â, æ̂, ê, î, ŀ̂, ô, ø̂, ơ̂, ƣ̂, ɍ̂, û, ư̂, and ŭ̂.

Dipping tone, known in Neph as tayŋdyƣ̃ƿƿǎ̇ņŝ (pronounced /tɑjŋ˧djoi̯w˦˨.wɑːɲ͜ʃ˥˩˦/), is written as a caron. For example: ǎ, æ̌, ě, ǐ, ŀ̌, ǒ, ø̌, ơ̌, ƣ̌, ɍ̌, ǔ, ư̌, and ŭ̌.

Transition tone, known in Neph as tayŋdyƣ̃ƿƿó̇v (pronounced /tɑjŋ˧djoi̯w˦˨.wɔːv˩˥/), is written as a perispomeni, which is visually indistinguishable from the tilde. The best way to describe this tone is that it's like a transition from high to low, but not as drastically or starkly as the falling tone. For example: ã, æ̃, ẽ, ĩ, ŀ̃, õ, ø̃, ỡ, ƣ̃, ɍ̃, ũ, ữ, and ŭ̃.

Geographic Spelling

Some letters in the Neph alphabet are written differently when presenting the names of places written in Neph to those that don't speak the language, such as on maps. This system, used by the International Organization for the Names of Countries And their Cities (IONCAC), is used on all maps, especially on those of Woidzthr. Those differences are listed below. Tone and length are NOT indicated under this system.

æ → ae, ĉ → ch, ð → dh, ģ → gy, ġ → gh, ķ → ky, ŀ → l or ll, ļ → ly, ņ → ny, ŋ → ng, ň → nh, ø → oe, ơ → ou, ƣ → oi, ɍ → r or rr, ŝ → sh, þ → th, ư → ue, ŭ → ui, ƿ → w, ẑ → zh, and ƶ → dz.

Input for humans

So, you might be thinking: “Okay, that's interesting and all, but how the hell am I supposed to type these?!” Well, human that's reading this page, there's a few ways to use the Neph alphabet on a computer.

The first is to use Unicode input. However, not all systems or environments support this, sadly. But, if you're on Linux, you can do CTRL + ALT + U, then the Unicode codepoint of the character, then RETURN.

Another way is to copy and paste the characters. This way is less likely to leave a head-shaped hole in your desk.
