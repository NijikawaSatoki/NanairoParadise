Zhuzuri Alphabets

Zhuzuri Alphabet (ælfab Zhüzurij)

The Zhuzuri alphabet (ælfab Zhüzurij /æɫˈɸab ʒyzʊˈrij/) has a whopping 41 letters. Those letters are:

A, æ, ä, b, c, ch, d, dh, e, f, g, gh, gj, h, i, j, k, kh, kj, l, lj, m, n, nj, o, œ, ö, õ, p, q, r, s, sh, t, th, u, ü, v, y, z, zh

As can be seen, the digraphs ch, dh, gh, gj, kh, lj, nj, sh, th, and zh are all classified as separate letters.

Diphthongs and Digraphs

Zhuzuri has 5 digraphs and 4 diphthongs.

Digraphs: dz, dzh, jv, ng, shch.

Diphthongs: ia, ie, io, iu

Consonants

Out of the 41 letters in the Zhuzuri alphabet, there are 29 consonants, with the digraphs contributing an additional 5. In other words, Zhuzuri has a total of 34 consonant sounds. Additionally, 13 of the total consonants can be ejective. So the real total number of consonants in Zhuzuri is actually 47.

Vowels

Zhuzuri has 12 vowels. Those vowels are:

A, æ, ä, e, i, o, œ, ö, õ, u, ü, y

As mentioned before, there are 4 digraphs representing soft vowels: ia, ie, io, and iu

There is no distinction between vowel length. All vowels are short, except for foreign names, which might indicate a long vowel by simply doubling it, i.e. õõ for Estonian õõ, or ææ for Finnish ää. This “long” vowel is still pronounced in Zhuzuri as a sequence of short vowels, however.

The vowel y tends to be pronounced /ɪ/ when the previous syllable has æ, ä, e, œ, ö, or ü; y tends to be pronounced /ɘ/ when the previous syllable has a, i, o, õ, or u. In the first syllable of a word, however, it seems random as to whether or not y gets pronounced /ɪ/ or /ɘ/. The phonemes /ɪ/ and /ɘ/ used to be spelled with two different letters, y and ŷ, but these have since merged into y.
